## 1. What the previously unread sources do in the first 20–70 instructions

### Takeaway

None of them treats the first few targets jointly, and none searches short program heads exhaustively as part of construction. The start-up is handled in one of three ways: a lazy palette priced at zero (Atlas's main constructor, identical to the base engine), a fixed ladder of squares physically built before the first target (Atlas's fallback and the Rust constructor: 1, 2, 3, 4, 16, 256, 65536, sometimes 2^32), or a greedy puzzle-specific choice of up to 12 extra dictionary values scored against all targets at once (`custom_palette` in the hosting repository's own planner, the only public code found that searches which values to create first). The depth-four and joint solvers are improvement passes on finished programs, and the seed grammar is a test tool, not a constructor.

### Cited Findings

**Atlas, main constructor (beam over a value graph)**

- It is the base engine again. Before any target it defines recipes for 1 (a start value divided by itself), the sixteen powers 2^k with k in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48} and the eleven odd numbers 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 23, 31, exactly the palette recorded in round two. Targets are sorted ascending and built one at a time. Leaves for each target are palette, start values, earlier targets largest first up to 48 leaves, then the newest created values up to 56; the two-step dictionary is rebuilt per target; beam width 10, rank `cost + 0.18 × log2(residual)`, 20 levels, binary doubling if nothing is found. Only values that some target needs are emitted. [src-part: lines 5040–5341] — [atlas.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/atlas.cpp)
- Because the palette alone can hold 28 values and there are up to 8 start values, the limit of 48 leaves room for only about 12–19 earlier targets, and 8 further slots for recent values. [derived from the same lines]
- Puzzles with a value limit of at most 300 and at most 6 targets go to an exact breadth-first search instead. [src-part: lines 351–372] — [atlas.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/atlas.cpp)

**Atlas, fallback constructor on the real register bank (used only when the beam returns nothing)**

- Start-up: build 1 and 2 always; then 3 if the largest target is at least 20, 4 if at least 64, 16 if at least 1,000, 256 if at least 100,000, 65536 if at least 10^8, and 2^32 only in the mode chosen when the largest target is at least 10^14. Each is one instruction (sum or square of the previous), so 7 or 8 instructions are spent before the first target. Unused ones are removed at the end by dead-code elimination. [src-part: lines 5558–5590, 5451, 5953] — [atlas.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/atlas.cpp)
- Target order: rebuild a table of all one-step values of the register contents and a sampled table of two-step values (at most 60,000); if any remaining target has an exact plan of at most 2 instructions, build the cheapest (ties to the smaller target); otherwise pick the target with the lowest difficulty estimate `min(bits, 2 + log2(distance to the nearest register or one-step value))`. [src-part: lines 5404–5453, 5752–5768]
- A target is built by "best approximation, then recurse on the residual": candidates are the nearest register, one-step and two-step values and products of a one-step value with a one- or two-step value near the quotient; each is rated by bits removed per instruction, those above 0.25 bits per instruction are kept (best 32), and the best 16 are compared by `plan cost + 1 + exact cost of the residual`, or `+ 0.45 × difficulty` when the residual has no exact plan. Recursion depth is 10, then binary doubling. [src-part: lines 5801–5930]
- Eviction protects the start-up pool explicitly: a register holding 1 gets +5000, a value up to 64 gets +2500, up to 4096 gets +800, the squares 4, 16, 256, 65536 get +2000, a start register +300; at most two values per 4-bit size band are protected (+3500, with a penalty for crowding); values within 50,000 or 1% of a remaining target, or within 50,000 of a small multiple (2 to 64) of it, get +8000 to +10000. [src-part: lines 5496–5556]

**`Juan-Severiano` rust/construct.rs (a fresh constructor that pays for everything on a real bank)**

- The header says the exact short joins, residual-rate planning and eviction are "inspired by" Atlas version 1. [src-full] — [construct.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Juan-Severiano/santa-2026/HEAD/rust/construct.rs)
- Start-up (`seed_palette`): build 1 and 2; then, depending on `seed mod 4`, one of four sets: nothing more; {3, 4, 16, 256, 65536}; that plus {5, 8, 10, 2^32}; or that plus {8, 32, 1024, 2^20, 2^32}; 2^32 is also added when the largest target is at least 10^13; a non-zero seed adds one pseudo-random odd number between 3 and 31 ("adds arithmetic diversity"). Only values not above the largest target are built, each with one instruction when possible and by binary doubling otherwise. A unit test fixes the cost: start value 7, target 10^9, seven instructions, 65536 present. [src-full] — [construct.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Juan-Severiano/santa-2026/HEAD/rust/construct.rs)
- Target order with a one-step look at the others: among the missing targets that are in the dictionary (at most 2 instructions), or failing that have an exact plan of at most 4, keep the 2 + effort cheapest; for each, execute the plan on a copy of the machine and score `own instructions + w × estimate for all other missing targets`, where a target one instruction away from the new bank counts 1 and any other counts `2 + 0.25 × log2(distance to the nearest bank value)`; w is 0.15, 0.30 or 0.45 by seed. If no target has such a plan, take the one minimising `log2(distance to the nearest two-step value) + 0.15 × log2(target)` and build it by approximation and residual recursion as in Atlas. [src-full]
- The dictionary is built from the register contents only (all pairs for one step; two-step values sampled with a stride, capped at 40,000 × effort and 160,000). Approximation candidates are priced by the distinct values that would really be emitted, so two two-step operands sharing a one-step helper cost four, not five. [src-full]
- Eviction mirrors Atlas: +2500 for values up to 64, +700 up to 4096, +1800 for 4, 16, 256, 65536, 2^32, size-band diversity, +9000 or +7000 for anchors near missing targets. [src-full]
- Result reported for this constructor in the repository README (read in round two, not re-read): 4,295 then 4,197 instructions on 20 puzzles against 2,837 for a 142k-level reference. [doc, via round-two notes] — [Juan-Severiano README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Juan-Severiano/santa-2026/HEAD/README.md)

**`custom_palette` in the hosting repository's planner (search over which values to create first)**

- Base palette here is smaller: 1, 2^k for k in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 16, 20, 32}, and 3, 5, 7, 11. [src-part: lines 815–820] — [virtual.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/solvers/native/virtual.cpp)
- Then a greedy selection of puzzle-specific values. Core = start values plus palette values up to 65536. Candidates = every one-step value of the core plus up to 520 randomly chosen two-step values of at least 9. For each target t and candidate h the code estimates the cost of t given h as the cheapest of: `1 + price(|t − h|)`; `1 + price(t / h)` if divisible; `2 + price(q) + price(r)` and `2 + price(q + 1) + price(h − r)` for quotient and remainder. `price(x)` is the exact cost when x is a one- or two-step value of the core, otherwise `2.1 + 0.118 × bits(x)`, lowered when x is one cheap gap away from a known value. Up to 12 rounds: choose the candidate with the largest `Σ over targets of min(2.6, current estimate − estimate with h) − 0.42 × cost(h)`, stop when the best gain is 0.18 or less; commit its recipe and add it to the palette. The comment states the intent: a candidate must improve "at least one still poorly represented target", and "several nearly interchangeable multipliers do not all receive the same credit". [src-part: lines 812–891] — [virtual.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/solvers/native/virtual.cpp)
- It is switched on by bit 512 of a mode word, and is skipped when bit 16 (palette reduced to the single value 1) is set. [src-part: lines 947–951]

Pseudocode in my own words:

```
custom_palette(puzzle):
    core = starts + small palette values
    cand = one-step values of core + sample of two-step values
    est[t] = min over h in core of project(t, h)          # cost estimate of target t
    repeat up to 12 times:
        for h in cand: gain[h] = sum_t min(2.6, est[t] - project(t, h)) - 0.42 * cost(h)
        h* = argmax gain;  if gain[h*] <= 0.18: stop
        add recipe of h* to the plan and to the palette
        est[t] = min(est[t], project(t, h*)) for all t
```

- The same planner has two other devices that touch the start. With a donor program and mode "style 3 to 6", it first commits every non-target value of the donor that serves at least 2, 4 or 6 targets (style 6 also requires three direct users) and re-plans the rest. With "tail k" it keeps the donor's recipes for all but the last k targets (donor order, shuffled, or grouped by overlap of dependency sets) and re-plans those k. [src-part: lines 893–942]
- "Meetings" (bit 256): during every pending target's backward search, residuals of at least 128 reached within 9 instructions are recorded with the up to three steps leading to them; a residual reached from two or more pending targets is a shared meeting. Meetings whose summed saving over the targets' cheapest individual plans is at least 3 are ranked by `saving − 0.14 × log2(value)`, the best 16 are searched, and each target's branch is attached when it adds fewer new values than that target's own plan. [src-part: lines 212–248, 1049–1075; the rest of this block was not read]
- Measured, 2026-11-20, against a 137,223 incumbent: warm planning with donor backbone, style 4, tail 8 and mode word 15949 saved 20 instructions on 17 puzzles with one seed and 15 on 15 with another; style 6, tail 12 saved 0 on 60 puzzles; the 40 longest puzzles with all moves enabled (mode word 16383) saved 0. A C# port without `custom_palette`, branching products and meetings averaged about 185 instructions per puzzle from scratch against about 137 for the incumbent. [doc] — [improve/NOTES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/improve/NOTES.md)
- Mode word 15949 has bit 512 set and bits 256 and 16 clear, so the 20- and 15-instruction results used `custom_palette` without meetings; 16383 sets bit 16, which replaces the palette by {1}. [derived from the bit tests in virtual.cpp]

**solver_depth4.cpp and solver_joint.cpp (`arsen123`)**

- Neither builds a program. Both read an existing program, turn it into a value graph, rewrite, and re-allocate registers with 24 or 48 goal orders. [src-full] — [solver_depth4.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/arsen_current/solver_depth4.cpp); [solver_joint.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/arsen_v7/solver_joint.cpp)
- Depth-four: for every computed value in program order (reversed on alternate rounds), remove it with the values only it needs; if that frees r values, look for a derivation from the retained values in fewer than r instructions, with a search matched to r: one step (r = 2), two steps including "square of a one-step value" and "one inverse step onto a one-step value" (r = 3), three steps (r = 4), or four steps of the shape `a × b ± d` with a, b, d each at most one step (r ≥ 5). A value whose removal frees only itself is skipped. [src-full]
- Joint: pivot on a non-target value with 2 to 4 users; the users become the outputs of a cut (a non-target output with a single user is replaced by that user, up to three times); at most 22 values are removed. First try one common new helper h such that every output is one instruction from h and a retained value (the candidate sets are intersected across outputs) and h itself costs at most 3; otherwise rebuild the outputs one after another in each rotation, each within 3 instructions, letting later outputs use earlier ones' new values. Accept when the graph gets smaller. [src-full]

**Seed grammar**

- `seed_grammar.cpp` enumerates every straight-line computation of at most 4 instructions (depth 2 to 4) from a few root values and records the shortest recipe found for each wanted value. Two prunings: the last instruction must use the most recently created value, and at depth one a pair of first values is tried once. [src-full] — [seed_grammar.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/seed_grammar.cpp)
- `seed_grammar.py` uses it as a test with controls. For each puzzle with a recovered "hub" model (values of at least 32 used at least twice as factors), it asks whether each hub is reachable within 4 instructions from the puzzle's start values, from another puzzle's start values with the same count (control), and within 3 instructions from the start values plus three peer hubs; each hub is paired with three random neighbours of similar size as a null. Output is a histogram of costs by 4-bit size band. [src-full] — [seed_grammar.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/seed_grammar.py)
- `hub_headers.py` turns the routes found into "headers" for the hub-based constructor: it picks one route per hub so that the routes are acyclic and the total `instructions used + estimate for hubs left unbuilt` is smallest, where an unbuilt hub of b bits is estimated at `(b − 2) / 2.1` instructions, clipped to between 2 and 10. [src-full] — [hub_headers.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/generative/hub_headers.py)
- The hub idea in the author's words: in puzzle 0006 all 32 targets can be written as `k × a × b + r` over four integers (261,389; 327,190; 571,027; 835,418); "building the shared integers first gives a chance to do fewer operations", with the cost of building the four from the start values counted. [doc] — [PROGRESS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/PROGRESS.md)
- Result for the hub route as a whole (2026-11-20): anchored hub reconstruction took a 137,108 candidate to 136,991; about 91 instructions over the first 170 of 530 models, then about 1 per 30 models, and 0 on 66 further puzzles. [doc] — [improve/NOTES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/improve/NOTES.md)

### Inferences

- (Inference) The public answer to "which values first" is almost always "none in particular": declare cheap generic candidates, price them at zero, and emit only what a chosen recipe uses. The two constructors that do pay up front (Atlas fallback, construct.rs) spend 7–8 instructions on one fixed chain of squares and are the weakest engines measured (construct.rs is about 50% above a 142k reference on 20 puzzles).
- (Inference) `custom_palette` is the closest public thing to what we are looking for. It differs from our failed "pre-build small values" in three ways: the candidates are one- and two-step values of this puzzle's own start values, not a generic list; each is credited by the estimated saving summed over all targets, with a cap of 2.6 per target so that interchangeable values are not all bought; and the price of the candidate is charged (0.42 per instruction, a deliberate under-charge). No ablation of it alone was found, so its value is unknown; the only numbers are for whole configurations.
- (Inference) The "meetings" device and the joint solver's common-helper step are the only public mechanisms that look for one value serving several targets before those targets are built. Both work on any pending targets, so they apply to the first few targets as a special case, but neither was measured there.
- (Inference) The depth-four pass rarely touches the head: early values have many users, so removing one frees only itself, which the pass skips. This matches our observation that local search cannot shorten the early part.

### Gaps

- No ablation of `custom_palette`, of meetings, or of Atlas's square ladder on the scored puzzles was found.
- The histograms produced by `seed_grammar.py` (how many hubs are reachable within 4 instructions, against the controls) were not found in any file read.
- Atlas's optimiser passes were not read, so whether any of them is aimed at the head is unknown; the header lists none that is.
- `konbu17`'s original planner and the notebooks themselves remain unread (kaggle.com not fetched).

## 2. Public experiments aimed at the start-up phase, with results and baselines

### Takeaway

Every public experiment that pays for a designed head up front was neutral or negative, and every experiment of any kind on the start-up phase returned zero on programs at or below about 134,000. The only sizeable gain came from a different move: lend a few values as free extra start registers during an improvement search, then pay for them and search again, which found 198 instructions on 840 puzzles of a 140,447 line against 55 for plain search with the same budget.

### Cited Findings

All experiments below are from `Hansen0223/Santa_2026`. "Independent" is that repository's own line of programs, "public-assisted" its merge with public programs. Dates are not printed in the files read; the baseline totals place them between 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-24.

**A. Pre-built heads for a constructor (official-heads)**

- Method. Run the Rudolph constructor once normally. Candidate head values are everything reachable from the start values in one step, in two steps (a one-step value combined with a start value or itself), in three steps (two one-step values combined), plus any value of the normal run whose own dependency set has at most 4 steps. "Directed" heads take values used by at least two targets in the normal run, ranked by `(targets + 1)^0.70 × (users + 1)^0.65 × bits^0.7 / path length^0.65`; "random" heads draw a control value with the same path length and the nearest bit length. Five heads per arm with 1, 2, 1, 2, 3 values. The head is executed first, its values are given to the constructor as extra start values, and the whole program is checked from the official start. [src-full] — [headers.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/collaboration/official-heads/headers.py)
- Result, totals after recipe selection and a fixed-effort graph rewrite. Four development puzzles: frozen independent 791; random heads 790; directed heads 795. ## 5. Lower bounds or estimates for the start-up cost

### Takeaway

Public lower bounds say very little about the start. The only start-specific terms are a size-growth count (the largest value can at most double or square per instruction, so 3 to 6 instructions are needed before a large target can exist) and a restriction on what the first two helper values can be. The proven total is 38,013 against 128,491 achieved. One exact result exists for a head: 28 instructions is minimal for supplying a given set of 25 small values from two start values.

### Cited Findings

- Growth bound. `prefix_bounds.py` computes, per puzzle, `targets not among the start values + max(0, growth steps − 1, helper bound)`, where the growth steps are counted by repeatedly replacing the largest start value by the larger of its double and its square until it reaches the smallest required target. [src-full] — [prefix_bounds.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/prefix_bounds.py)
- First-two-helpers restriction. The same file limits the first helper to values one instruction from the start values (after greedily producing every target that is directly reachable), and the second helper to values one instruction from start values plus one first helper; these domains cap how many targets the first two helpers can be credited with in the counting bound. [src-full] — [prefix_bounds.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/prefix_bounds.py)
- The counting bound in the author's words: for each target, check whether start values or other targets produce it directly, then compute how many remaining targets a fixed number of extra intermediate values can cover at most. [doc] — [README.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/README.md)
- Totals. Proven lower bound over the 1,000 puzzles: 38,013 (2026-11-24, after several additions that day from 37,988); 21 puzzles proven optimal, together 151 instructions. Earlier: 33,331 on 2026-11-19 with 16 puzzles optimal. [doc] — [PROGRESS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/PROGRESS.md); [README.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/README.md)
- Exact result for one head. For puzzle 0649, a search from the start values 6 and 29 with every created value kept found a feasible order with three helper values after 13,477 nodes and exhausted the budget of two helpers after 246 nodes: "producing this fixed set of 25 boundary values from these two start values needs at least 28 steps". The README adds that the whole puzzle's 118 is only an upper bound and that "changing the boundary values supplied downstream may still shorten it". The method was cross-checked against a breadth-first enumeration on 105 small random instances. [doc] — [exact-boundary README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/collaboration/exact-boundary/README.md)
- A related tool keeps a prefix of an incumbent's helper values (in program order, by use count, or randomised), eagerly adds every target that becomes one step away, and asks an exact engine whether the remaining targets can be completed within a budget of 2 to 4 further helpers; it is restricted to programs of at most 31 instructions. No result for it was found in the files read. [src-full] — [prefix_exact.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026/HEAD/solvers/proofs/prefix_exact.py)
- Guide's counting argument for later targets: one instruction can reach at most about 2 × 48² ≈ 4,608 new values, about 12 bits, so "a 60-bit target therefore needs at least ~5 instructions even from an ideal register set, and the best programs spend ~7.0 on the largest bin". [doc] — [guide notebook v217](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AsteriosPanoras/kaggle-portfolio/HEAD/competitions/santa-2026/notebooks/santa-2026-best-public-score-dataset-fails/v217.ipynb)

### Inferences

- (Inference) The growth bound is tiny in practice. From a largest start value near 2,000 (11 bits) three squarings pass 60 bits; from a start value of 2 it takes six. So the public bound on instructions before a large first target is 2 to 5 helpers, against the 21 to 37 observed.
- (Inference) Applying the guide's counting argument to the start gives a somewhat stronger, still unpublished estimate. With m values available an instruction has at most about 2m² outcomes. Starting from 4 values, the number of distinct values reachable as the last result of n instructions is at most the product of 2(4 + i)² for i from 0 to n − 1, which first exceeds 2^60 at n = 9; starting from 1 value, at n = 11. A generic 60-bit first target therefore needs on the order of 9 to 11 instructions. This is a crude information count (it ignores the value limit and overcounts), and it is far below our 37.5 for puzzles with 56–63-bit median targets. The difference is the part of the head that exists to serve later targets, not the first one.
- (Inference) Subtracting the 26,898 target instructions from the proven total of 38,013 leaves about 11,100 helper instructions proven necessary, about 11 per puzzle, against about 101,600 helper instructions in the 128,491 merge. Lower bounds therefore give no usable target for how short a head could be.
- (Inference) The 0649 result suggests a usable diagnostic of our own: for a small or medium puzzle, take the set of small values the rest of our program consumes and ask an exact or strong search for the cheapest program producing that set from the start values. If our heads are within one or two instructions of that, as the public one was, effort on "how the head is computed" is wasted and the question becomes which set to ask for.

### Gaps

- No public source states a lower bound or an estimate specifically for the cost of the first target or of the first few targets beyond the growth count.
- The stronger graph-based bounds in the repository (`distinct-helper-edges`, `distinct-diamond`, `subset-capacity`) were not read; the status table says the "ordinary prefix" form of one of them certifies 113 puzzles, without saying what it implies for the start.
- No result file was found for `prefix_exact.py`.
